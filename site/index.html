Ask your Mac.
It points the way.

Cursor Pilot is a small helper that lives next to your mouse cursor. Hold ⌃⌥ anywhere and talk. It looks at your screens, answers out loud, and a glass buddy cursor flies over to point at what you need.

Get it on GitHub Build it yourself

macOS 14 or later · best on macOS 26 · uses your own Anthropic API key

Hold two keys, ask out loud, and it points at the answer or does the task for you.

  1. 1

    Hold ⌃⌥ and talk

    Control + Option, from any app. No window to find, no box to type in. Speech recognition runs on your Mac with Apple's speech model on macOS 26.

  2. 2

    It looks at your screens

    It sees what you see, across your displays, so you can ask about the thing in front of you without describing it.

  3. 3

    It shows you, or does it

    It answers out loud with the best voice installed on your Mac, and the buddy cursor flies over to point. Or it just gets the job done for you.

Circles, arrows, highlights

Besides pointing, it can circle, arrow and highlight things right on your screen.

Draw to ask ⌃⌥D

Scribble on your screen, then ask about your drawing. "What's this?" works a lot better with a circle around it.

A chat like texting

Short replies, your history, search, and your drawings, in a chat window that feels like a messaging app.

Lives in the notch

A small notch panel shows when it's listening, thinking or answering, so you always know where it's at.

Like a co-worker who can use your computer.

Ask for something done and it does it, quietly, in the background. You give consent once, during onboarding. After that there are no permission prompts, and it tells you what it's doing as it goes.

  • Runs shell commands for the quick stuff.
  • Reads and edits files, backing up each edit first.
  • Scripts your apps with AppleScript.
  • Uses the mouse and keyboard when it has to.

When it's just using the shell, the buddy stays out of your way.

Get it on GitHub

Build it yourself

$ git clone <repo>
$ cd cursor-pilot
$ scripts/build-app.sh

Needs Xcode and Rust. The finished app lands in ~/Applications. Requires macOS 14 or later; best on macOS 26 with Liquid Glass.

Common questions

What does it cost?

Cursor Pilot is free and open source under the MIT license. It talks to Claude by Anthropic using your own API key, so you pay Anthropic directly, and only for what you use. There's no subscription and nothing to pay us.

Where does my data go?

Requests go straight from your Mac to Anthropic. There's no account, no server of ours and no tracking. On macOS 26, speech recognition runs on-device with Apple's speech model, and replies are spoken with the best system voice you have installed.

What can it touch on my Mac?

With your one-time consent during onboarding, it can run shell commands, read and edit files (backing up each edit), script apps with AppleScript, and use the mouse and keyboard when it has to. It works in the background without permission prompts and says what it's doing as it goes.

Which macOS versions does it run on?

macOS 14 or later. It's at its best on macOS 26, with Liquid Glass and on-device speech recognition.

How do I install it?

Head to the GitHub repository, or build it yourself: clone the repo and run scripts/build-app.sh. You'll need Xcode and Rust.
